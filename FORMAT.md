# File format

What ScatterSteg writes into an image, exactly enough to write a compatible decoder. Until the first release it may still change: an image made by one commit may not open with a later one. The implementations are `codec.c` (C) and `rust/src/codec.rs` (Rust), and `tests/test_cli.py` checks both against an independent Python decoder.

## Layout

The image contains no header or version marker, as either would reveal the presence of a message. Every sample (the R, G and B values of a pixel, or its gray value) takes part with its two lowest bits: the salt and the length region (steps 1 and 4) are read from the LSBs and written by flipping them (the encoder pairs those few changes into value swaps where it can, and otherwise nudges ±1), and the box (step 5) is read from both lowest bits, with the sign of each ±1 change carrying bits too. Below, "sample k" means bit position k counted along the payload order: pixel `order[k / channels]`, channel `k % channels`.

1. **Salt** — 16 random bytes, carried as the STC syndrome (as in step 5, with the same costs) of the samples of a public *salt region*: the first k pixels of a public pixel order, `prng_permutation` keyed with `BLAKE2b-256("ScatterSteg v2 salt positions")`, where k = max(⌈128 / channels⌉, min(⌈8192 / channels⌉, ⌊pixels / 16⌋)) and the first min(k · channels, 1024 · 128) of its samples are used. Public, so the decoder can find it before it has a key; syndrome-coded, so the roughly 18 changes it needs are as sparse as the payload's.
2. **Keys** — `master = Argon2id(passphrase, salt, opslimit 3, memlimit 256 MiB)`; the scatter key and encryption key are `crypto_kdf` subkeys 1 and 2 of `master`, context `ScatStg2`.
3. **Order** — `prng_permutation(scatter key)` over the pixels, with the salt pixels removed.
4. **Length region** — 64 bits: the box length L (32 bits, LSB first), then s, how many of the box's bits are in layer A (32 bits, LSB first; step 5). They are the STC syndrome (as in step 5, with m = 64) of the LSBs of the first K samples, where K = min(2048, max(64, ⌊S / 16⌋)) and S is the number of samples in the order (positions × channels). Syndrome-coded, like the salt, so its changes go where they are cheap: written straight into 32 samples, the length took about 16 changes wherever those fell, which an off-the-shelf detector picked up on its own.
5. **Box** — `crypto_secretbox(encoding (1) || body)` with a nonce of 24 zero bytes, as bits (LSB first), m = 8L of them, carried in two layers over the same samples (ternary embedding: a change of +1 or −1 flips a sample's LSB either way, and its second bit in exactly one direction, +1 from an odd value and −1 from an even one, so the direction carries bits too). With N samples left after the K of step 4, u = min(N, 1024 · ⌈m / 2⌉) of them are used, and the box's first s bits are *layer A*, the other m − s bits *layer B*. Layer A is the STC syndrome of the second bits `⌊v / 2⌋ mod 2` of the first n_A = min(u, 1024 · s) samples (floor division, so for a negative coefficient too); layer B is the STC syndrome of the LSBs of the first n_B = min(u, 1024 · (m − s)) samples; a layer with no bits has no samples. A layer of m′ bits over n samples is coded like the salt and the length region: message bit i is row i of H·y, where block i covers samples ⌊i·n/m′⌋ to ⌊(i+1)·n/m′⌋ − 1 and its j-th sample contributes column j of the 7-row submatrix to rows i…i+6. Columns: splitmix64 seeded with `0x5354432d48415431 ^ (⌊n/m′⌋ + 1)`, low 7 bits, with bits 0 and 6 set. The rows run in independent chunks of ⌊2²⁰ / (⌊n/m′⌋ + 1)⌋ message bits. A decoder refuses a box when L is under 16, m > N or s > m. Any s from 0 to m is valid: the encoder picks s from the costs (0.50 of the box in tests from 0.03 to 0.5 bits per sample, in both builds), and lowers it, down to 0 (layer B alone, plain ±1 embedding), where layer A can't carry its share (a cover of mostly 0s or 255s, whose second bits no ±1 change flips, or a message near capacity). The nonce isn't stored: every message draws a fresh random salt, so its encryption key is used once, and a fixed nonce is safe. (Until 2026-11-01 a random 24-byte nonce came first, and before the ternary box the whole box was one binary layer; images made before then don't decode now.)

**The message** is carried in a *record*: `name length (1) || file name || message`, with a name length of 0 for no name. The name, when there is one, is a bare file name of 1–255 bytes of UTF-8 (no overlong forms or surrogates), not starting with `.`, with no `/`, no control characters (U+0000–U+001F, U+007F–U+009F) and no bidirectional controls (U+061C, U+200E, U+200F, U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069). A sender could otherwise plant a hidden file such as `.profile` in the recipient's directory, or a name that prints as something else. The secretbox's plaintext is `encoding (1) || body`: with encoding 1, the body is the record as raw DEFLATE (RFC 1951, no zlib or gzip wrapper), used when that is shorter than the record; with encoding 0, the body is the record as is. Any other encoding, a body that isn't exactly one complete DEFLATE stream or that inflates past 64 MiB + 256 bytes, a name length past the record's end, or a name that breaks the rules above is malformed, and decoders refuse it. An encoder may compress however it likes; ScatterSteg uses zlib, at level 9 unless `-z` says otherwise.

**JPEG** uses the same layout over a different kind of position: each *carrier* is one AC coefficient, whatever its value (zeros included), taken in component, block-row, block and coefficient order (63 per 8×8 block), and carries bits as above. Its salt region is counted in carriers (one sample each). DC coefficients are never used. Embedding keeps every carrier within ±1023, so the encoder can write it; the decoder finds the same carriers whatever the changes did to their values. (Until 2026-10-31 only AC coefficients with |value| ≥ 2 were carriers, and the length was written straight into samples 0–31; images made before either change don't decode now.)

**Costs** determine which samples change and are chosen by the encoder alone (the decoder does not use them). PNG uses HILL: per channel, the reciprocal of the 3×3 mean of the absolute KB high-pass residual, smoothed by a 15×15 mean, so a change is cheap where the image is busy. JPEG uses UERD: the coefficient's quantization step over the energy of its block (the sum of its dequantized AC magnitudes) plus a quarter of each of its eight neighbours' (a block with no AC energy around it gets a large finite cost instead). Builds made for benchmarks can use other models instead (WOW or S-UNIWARD for PNG, J-UNIWARD for JPEG; see `distortion.h`); as the decoder never sees the costs, what they write reads back with any build.

`prng_permutation(key, n)` is a forward Fisher–Yates shuffle (for `i` from 0 to n−2, swap slot `i` with `i + uniform(n − i)`), with `uniform` drawing little-endian 32-bit words from the ChaCha20-IETF keystream of `key` (zero nonce, counter from 0) and rejecting values below `2³² mod bound`.
